# Level visibility

What decides which track faces, sky and kart wheels are drawn far away, and what the native version changes. Read this before touching level culling in `game/MAIN/MainFrame.c`, `game/MAIN/MainFrame_RenderFrame.c`, the level renderers (`game/226` to `game/229`), `game/DrawSky.c` or `game/DrawTires.c`.

## The stages
A level face is drawn only if it passes every stage, in this order:

1. **Visibility lists (PVS).** Each quadblock can carry precomputed lists: the BSP leaves and quadblocks visible from it, nearby level instances, and the water and vertex-colour vertices to animate. The camera picks the lists of the quadblock under it (`CAM.c`, after `CAM_FindClosestQuadblock`), and `MainFrame_VisMemFullFrame` copies them into `gGT->visMem1`. **Native ignores them for level geometry**: `MainFrame_VisMemFullFrame` sets every leaf, quadblock, water vertex and vertex-colour vertex visible each frame ([why](#why-the-lists-are-off)). The exception is a BSP node that no quadblock's list ever shows: it is never drawn from anywhere, so it stays hidden (`MainFrame_BuildEverVisibleLeafs`, the union of all lists, built per level by `MainInit_VisMem`). On the 18 retail race tracks that is one empty leaf of Dingo Canyon and nothing else; converted tracks put their collision-only blocks there ([TRACK-CONVERTER.md](TRACK-CONVERTER.md#after-crashteameditor)). Instances keep their lists, because warp pads (`AH_WarpPad.c`) and cutscenes (`CS_Instance.c`) read them as gameplay state.
2. **Frustum.** The BSP walk drops leaves outside the view. Widescreen widens it (`Widescreen_ScaleFrustumX`).
3. **Distance.** Far leaves and quadblocks drop out, and textures and models change LOD by distance. `increase_draw_distance` moves all these limits out by the same factor, 4 (`include/platform/native_draw_distance.h`): the frustum's far clip (`PushBuffer.c`), the depth where a BSP leaf switches to the cheaper full-dynamic emitter (1P and split screen), a model's LOD distance, and the depths that pick a level texture's LOD and a quadblock's subdivision. The last two go together: a quadblock's full-resolution texture only loads in the deepest subdivision. One factor for all, because the nearest limit decides what the player sees disappear. The exception is a level whose `configFlags` carry `LEVEL_CONFIG_ONE_TEXTURE_LOD` (converted tracks, [TRACK-CONVERTER.md](TRACK-CONVERTER.md#after-crashteameditor)): it has the same texture at every LOD, so deeper subdivision shows nothing new under PGXP's perspective texturing, and its LOD, subdivision and full-dynamic depths keep retail's values (`NativeDrawDistance_LevelLod`); only its far clip moves out. On an open converted course nearly every block falls inside the scaled depths: with them, Figure-8 Circuit took three and a half times as long per frame with the far camera; with retail's depths the image is the same but for single texels where faces split differently.
4. **Near clip.** Faces crossing the near plane are written as clip records and clipped after the rest of the level, but only when they reach the view (`DrawLevelOvr1P_ShouldWriteRenderedClippedRecord`); otherwise the face is dropped whole. A face goes this way when a corner is nearer than the screen distance; only the part nearer than half of it is cut away. Retail asks for a corner inside a box half the screen distance in front of the camera, sized for 4:3. Native tests the face against the view frustum in view space (the level's view-projection matrix, so widescreen included): it is dropped only when every corner is outside one plane, nearer than the cut or past one screen edge (8 pixels of margin). A converted course's large floor faces broke the box both ways: a face crossing the camera plane beside the camera, and a face running from beside the camera far into the view, had every corner outside it, and the floor at the side of the frame showed the clear colour, the grass or the stands under the road. A face the frustum test drops draws nothing, so retail demos are pixel-identical. The records go into each player's share of the native clip scratch (`NativeClipScratch_PlayerEntries`, `platform/native_clip_scratch.h`), not retail's per-track budget (`MainDB_GetClipSize`, 12,000 bytes on a race track): a frame whose records pass the end of the buffer loses all of them (`DrawLevelOvr1P_ConsumeClipRecords`), which is the floor next to the camera vanishing, and every writer checks for space. A dense converted course with a widescreen far camera writes 19,500 bytes; retail tracks fit retail's budget, and their frames are unchanged. The clipped faces are drawn however large: the PS1 GPU skips a triangle 1024 or more pixels wide (`NativeGpu_HardwareSkipsTriangle`, tested at its 4:3 width), which hides faces crossing the camera plane unclipped, but these the game clipped itself, and widescreen, the far camera and a converted course's large faces make pieces wider than that. Skipped, they left the floor by the camera showing the clear colour or what lies under the road (`NativeGpu_AddLevelClippedRange`). Retail demos are pixel-identical with or without the exception.

## Why the lists are off
The lists were baked for the retail view: 4:3, the retail draw distance and the camera positions the developers sampled. With widescreen, the far camera (`L2`) or `increase_draw_distance`, the camera sees past them, so stretches of far road and scenery vanish until the camera reaches a quadblock with other lists. A split-screen camera can even lose the floor under its kart.

They only saved PS1 time. Faces hidden behind nearer ones are still drawn, but the ordering table puts them first, so nearer faces paint over them. The cost is primitive-arena space and frame time:
- Arena peak per frame at 16:9 with the far camera: 120 to 292 KB per track without the lists (79 to 130 KB with). With `increase_draw_distance` too: up to 586 KB in 1P, 820 KB in two-player split screen (N. Gin Labs) and 365 KB in four-player. The arena is 2 MB (`CTR_NATIVE_PRIM_ARENA_BYTES`, `platform/native_memory.c`). A dev run logs `[CTR Dev] primitive arena peak`.
- With `increase_draw_distance` on N. Gin Labs, the heaviest track: about 1.5 ms a frame more than the retail limits in 1P and 2.7 ms in 2P. The option is off by default, Steam Deck included.

## The sky
The sky (`game/DrawSky.c`) is a dome of large gouraud triangles around the camera, in eight 45-degree slices. Retail draws the four slices facing the camera and drops a face whole when the GTE flags a corner too close to the camera plane, and the GPU drops any triangle 512 or more pixels tall (`NativeGpu_HardwareSkipsTriangle`). From the far camera and in widescreen, a face still covering a screen corner hits one of those, and the clear colour shows through.

Native draws all eight slices and sends every face through `DrawSky_ClipFace`: clipped in view space against the near plane and the screen edges (8 pixels outside), projected through the same view as the level (the precise one under PGXP), and drawn as a fan with colours interpolated at the cuts, so pieces stay within the GPU's limits. Every face goes this way, not only those retail would drop, so neighbours share bit-identical corners (mixing GTE-projected and clipped faces leaves hairline seams).

## Distant wheels
A kart's tires are four camera-facing sprites (`game/DrawTires.c`), drawn only at the kart's top model LOD. Their centres are taken relative to the camera at 4 times world scale and go through the GTE's 16-bit vectors, which hold about 8,000 world units; with `increase_draw_distance` a top-LOD kart can be farther. Such a wheel keeps its full-width centre (the GTE's 32-bit MAC1 to MAC3, not the saturated IR1 to IR3), takes its facing from it, and has its corners projected by `NativeGte_ProjectWide` (`platform/native_gte_core.c`), an RTPS with a 32-bit input that advances the same registers and PGXP state. Wheels that fit keep the retail GTE path bit for bit. Packing an out-of-range corner into 16 bits would wrap it behind the camera and draw a long dark sliver across the screen.

## Checking it
- `CTR_DEV_RETAIL_CULL=1` keeps the retail lists and sky, for A/B captures ([DEV-TOOLS.md](DEV-TOOLS.md)). Every differing pixel should be far geometry the lists dropped or sky that was missing, never a near face overdrawn. The clear colour must not show in any frame with the sky; Hot Air Skyway at `CTR_DEV_WARP=10799,325,-2381,2271` with the far camera shows it with the retail sky and not without.
- Distant wheels: where wheels fit, no pixel changes. On Dingo Canyon (frame 3390 of a `CTR_DEV_RACE=0` autopilot run) the far karts have wheels; `CTR_DEV_DUMP_FRAME` lists them as `@tires` quads next to the karts' polygons.
- A spot a player reports: they press `F7` there, and `where.txt` in the capture gives the `CTR_DEV_WARP` line. Warp there with `CTR_DEV_PAD=2400:2` for the far camera and capture both ways.
