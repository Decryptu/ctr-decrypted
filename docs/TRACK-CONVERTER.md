# Track converter: Mario Kart DS courses

`tools/tracks/` turns a course from the player's own Mario Kart DS ROM into a custom track package ([CUSTOM_TRACKS.md](CUSTOM_TRACKS.md#3-the-package-format)). Nothing from the ROM or the CTR disc is in this repository; the tools read them and write the package on the player's machine. A package holds the course's models and textures, and the crate and fruit models from the disc.

Status: all 32 race courses convert, with their item boxes, Wumpa fruit, minimap and scenery. All finish laps on autopilot; `ROADMAP.md` lists what is open. Play-testing finds visual and surface bugs the checks cannot see. Objects move and hit as in MKDS ([Movers](#movers)); models play MKDS's joint animations where `ANIMATED` lists them, and textures are not animated.

## Use
1. Build CrashTeamEditor's headless converter once, from an MSYS2 shell:
   `MSYSTEM=MINGW32 /c/msys64/usr/bin/bash.exe -lc "bash tools/tracks/cte/build.sh C:/t/cte"`.
   It clones [CrashTeamEditor](https://github.com/mateusfavarin/CrashTeamEditor) (MIT) at a pinned commit, applies `crashteameditor.patch`, adds `headless.cpp` and builds `C:\t\cte\cte_headless.exe`. Python needs `numpy` and `Pillow`.
2. Convert: `python tools/tracks/mkds_convert.py <rom.nds> cross_course --title "Figure-8 Circuit" --disc <ctr-u.bin>`. `--disc` is the player's CTR (USA) disc image, which the crate and fruit models come from. It writes the OBJ, LEV and probe points to `C:\t\mkds-cross_course` and installs the package in its `packages` folder, printing the pin. The same input always gives the same pin. `--store <install>\assets\tracks\packages` installs into a game folder instead.
3. Check: `tools\tracks\check_track.ps1 -Rom <rom.nds> -Course cross_course`. It races three laps on autopilot, hidden and fast-forwarded (`devrun.ps1`), and prints `PASS` or what failed. Frames land in `<work>\shots`.

`tools/tracks/mkds_batch.py <rom.nds> --disc <ctr-u.bin> --check [--version X.Y.Z] [course ...]` does both for every race course (`--version` is the package version they all get: the catalogue gives every track one), four races at a time, and prints a line for each; each course's output is in `convert.log` and `check.log` in its work folder. A full run takes about 30 minutes on an otherwise idle machine, and much longer beside a running game.

## What the converter writes
Scale: MKDS course models are drawn 16 times their stored size; one MKDS world unit is 1/12 of a CrashTeamEditor unit, and one of those is 64 LEV units.

**Drawn mesh** (`course_model.nsbmd`, materials with no collision flags):
- Textures are decoded from `course_model.nsbtx`. A level texel is solid or clear, so only one nearly solid in MKDS (`ALPHA_SOLID`) is solid here. A translucent decal on the floor (`find_overlays`: Waluigi Pinball's arrow panels, tree shadows) is blended in its texture over what lies under it, and if MKDS draws it in the plane of that floor it rises `OVERLAY_LIFT` (3 MKDS units: at 1, the floor under Waluigi Pinball's lanes won the depth test in specks at a distance), as the level is depth-tested. A wide texture that 16 colours damage (`WIDE_ROUGH`, Rainbow Road's gradients) takes 256 at half the width. A repeating texture is tiled inside its image (up to 4 times, as far as one triangle spans it) while everything fits 14 texture pages of the VRM. Textures wider than 128 pixels are quantised to 16 colours, transparency included, because a texture page holds 128 pixels at 8 bits, and a side over 256 pixels is halved, as a page is 256 texels each way. CrashTeamEditor refuses an image past either limit and gives its material an all-zero texture layout: such a block samples the framebuffer at VRAM (0, 0), so it flickers in whatever colour was last drawn there.
- Triangles are cut at texture-tile edges, then split until no edge is longer than `--max-edge` (24 units, 1536 LEV units; retail quadblocks span about 900 to 1600).
- Corners within 1.5 LEV units are welded. A corner on a surface's rim that lies on another triangle's open edge without being its corner (a tile cut one side has and the other does not) would leave a hairline of sky once both sides round to the LEV grid, so `overlap_tjunctions` moves it 1.5 LEV units across that edge, into the neighbour. The level is depth-tested, so the overlap does not show. Inserting the corner into the neighbour instead closes a little more but costs 50% more blocks on N64 Choco Mountain, where this version has 6% fewer. To measure a change, compare captures from fixed `CTR_DEV_CAMERA` positions with the kart out of shot: a racing kart does not end up in the same place in two packages.
- Two triangles that share an edge become one quad when one texture mapping covers both, the shape is convex and it is flat within 2 LEV units: the quad's midpoints and centre then get exactly the texture the triangles had. Four quads around one vertex become one quadblock, each face keeping its own UVs, when each outer edge's middle vertex is on the straight line between its corners (within 2 LEV units): far away the game draws a block as one quad over its four corners, while the neighbours keep that middle vertex as a corner, so on a curved hill the sky showed through the triangle between. What is left becomes triblocks. Fewer, larger blocks matter because the level renderer queues the blocks near the camera in a bounded 1,024-pointer list (including its terminator) and does not draw what does not fit ([below](#checks)).
- CrashTeamEditor takes a block's facing from its corners 0 and 2 and turns the block over when that disagrees with the OBJ normal. A quad that undoes a midpoint split has a straight corner, which gives it a zero normal and a facing chosen at random; a block turned over vanishes from the front, as back faces are culled (the road under the kart showed black). Each quad's ring starts so that corners 0 and 2 are not straight, a grid is only made when none of its outer corners is, and the normal written is that of the diagonals.
- Materials MKDS draws from both sides (polygon attribute bit 6: fences, rails, signs, the giant blocks behind the walls) are double-sided quadblocks (CrashTeamEditor's `_drawflags`).

**Collision** (`course_collision.kcl`): invisible quadblocks carrying the terrain and flags (table `COLLISION` in `mkds_convert.py`, and `KCL_MUD` for the attribute variants that are mud or dirt, which take CTR's `Mud` terrain, Tiny Arena's, with its splashes, its double friction off on a converted course (`VehPhysForce.c`), where it would stop a kart that lets go of the gas; CrashTeamEditor's flag labels are misleading, the script names the game's bits). The engine tests each LEV triangle exactly, with no radius to hide gaps, so the collision mesh is made watertight before it is written. Ground a kart drives on is never `Water` or `Fast Water`: on those the game clips the kart's model at world height 0, where retail levels have them (`COLL.c`), and on a converted course the kart vanishes. MKDS's heavy sand (0x05) is `Slow Dirt`, its deep mud (0x06: Luigi's Mansion's bog, SNES Choco Island 2's) `Mud`, and its shallow water (0x11) `River Asphalt`:
- vertices within 0.5 MKDS units are welded, and a vertex lying on another triangle's edge is inserted into that edge (T-junctions leave slivers a kart falls through);
- long edges are split at their midpoints in every triangle that shares them, so neighbours stay matched (large triangles left the kart under the road);
- positions are snapped to the LEV grid (1/64 unit) before writing, so the mesh checked is the one the game tests. Corners closer than 2 LEV units are then welded: splitting two near-parallel source edges leaves midpoints closer than the first weld, and a shared corner moves the same in every triangle, so welding opens no gap;
- a triangle thinner than 3 LEV units (`SLIVER_WIDTH`) is closed: each neighbour across its longest edge is split at its far corner, so its two short edges take its place in the mesh (`close_slivers`). Dropped instead, it would leave a strip with no ground;
- a triangle the grid still turned more than 25 degrees from its source triangle (`TURNED_COS`) is dropped: turned over, the game would test it facing down, and stood up across a road it is a wall that stops the kart dead.

**Track data** (`course_map.nkm`): one lap of the CPU route, as `EPOI` holds its sections in no driving order and `EPAT` links them, forking where a course does (`cpu_route` follows each section's first link until the route closes); eight spawns behind the start, facing along the route (a kart at game angle `a` faces `(sin a, cos a)` in x/z, and `VehBirth` turns a spawn's `rot.y` a quarter turn before using it), the finish line (collision triangles crossing it are split so the lap checkpoints start exactly on it), three AI lanes along the CPU route (its centre line, and 40% of its radius to each side, on the ground, as a CPU kart holds its lane's height), a sky gradient from the most colourful texture of `course_model_V` (three bands in retail's order: band 0 highest, each from its upper edge to its lower one, the last running 100 pixels below the horizon; `CAM_SkyboxGlow` skips a band written the other way up when PGXP is off), and the probe points `check_track.ps1` uses (every 64 LEV units on every MKDS ground layer).

**Turbo pads** (KCL types 0x07 and 0x12): collision blocks of these types are listed in a `turbopad.json` preset, and CrashTeamEditor raises a copy of each 32 LEV units as its trigger (flags `0xa040`, terrain 1, as retail's). Type 0x12, MKDS's boost ramps on the jumps of Cheep Cheep Beach, Wario Stadium, Waluigi Pinball and Bowser Castle, is a super turbo pad. The kart's sphere reaches it while it drives on the road, which gives the pad's 960 reserves (`VEH_PHYS_FORCE_TURBO_PAD_RESERVES`). What the pad looks like is the MKDS road texture.

**Jump pads** (KCL type 0x0c): road, with a trigger block over each like a turbo pad's. CrashTeamEditor knows only the two turbo triggers, so `lev_patch.py` sets those blocks' terrain to `COLL_STEP_TRIGGER_JUMP_PAD_HIGH`, which a converted track reads as the game's forced high jump, the one Mystery Caves' turtles give (`VehPhysForce.c`). GBA Bowser Castle 2 and GBA Sky Garden have them, each over a gap a kart at speed clears, and SNES Choco Island 2's mounds are small ones.

### Flights
What CTR's physics cannot do, the kart is carried through on a scripted path: it has no collision, nothing can hit it (`VehPickState`), and it lands with its speed, thrown by no climb at the path's end (`game/ConvertedTrack/VehFlight.c`). `find_flights` in `mkds_convert.py` makes them from the course's data, so any course with one of these gets it, and prints each:
- **Cannons** (KCL type 0x0f, a wall with no collision): from the wall, in an arc, to the first ground on the route past the point in the NKM's `KTPC` section. `clear_arc` takes the arc of `CANNON_ARCS` that passes through least of what is drawn, roofs included.
- **Loops** (KCL type 0x13): a triangle facing up less than `LOOP_ROAD` has no collision, and the path runs on the type's surface, the kart's up its normal, so the kart goes over upside down, on until the road faces up `LOOP_LEVEL`, where the kart runs out along it. The kart keeps its own speed and its heading stays while it is not upright, so the camera does not swing at the top. The gentler parts are drivable. At each exit, `loop_exit_walls` finds the seam with the steep mesh and puts a one-sided collision wall there to keep a kart driving backwards from running off the drivable surface.
- **Gaps**: route over no ground at all for `FLIGHT_GAP` or more, past what a kart jumps.
- **Steps**: ground rising `FLIGHT_STEP` at once, which only a moving floor climbs in MKDS.

`lev_patch.py` writes them to the level's `ptrSpawnType2_PosRot`, as `VehFlight.c` describes. A flight starts when the kart's movement crosses the gate plane going forward, within the gate's width and height at the crossing. The final path segment sets the exit heading and seeds driving's speed and movement angles, which driving uses to rebuild velocity on its first frame. A CPU kart holds its lane, and the lanes run along the same path (`flight_paths` in `convert`), upright and at the kart's own speed.

### CPU karts
A CPU kart follows its lane's nodes and never rises above them, so what it does comes from the lanes in `<course>_nav.json`. They lie on the ground and clear it between two nodes, where a kart runs straight, as under it the depth test hides the kart, ramps included (a ramp may rise `LANE_REACHES_RAMP` over MKDS's route), over a gap at the route's height. `flags` gives each node's `NavFrame` flags: a boost pad or ramp under a lane, looked for `LANE_SAMPLES` times between two nodes as a pad is shorter than they are apart, is a turbo or a super turbo (`LANE_FLAGS`). Off a ramp or jump pad a lane rises in the arc a kart flies, `KART_SPEED` under `KART_GRAVITY`, until it meets the ground.

### What must fit
Limits differ from course to course, and `main` and `fit_to_lev` meet them:
- **Near blocks**: the game's near-camera list holds 256 blocks (`check_track.ps1`). `MAX_EDGE` in `mkds_batch.py` gives the courses that show more of themselves at once a larger `--max-edge`: Waluigi Pinball and Airship Fortress.
- **Vertices**: a quadblock indexes vertices with 16 bits, and past 65,535 CrashTeamEditor's indices wrap and scramble its blocks. Conversion starts at `--max-edge` and raises it by a quarter until the OBJ has under 65,000 (`MAX_VERTICES`). 7 courses need this, 37.5 at most, on Peach Gardens.
- **Texture space**: when CrashTeamEditor writes no VRM, conversion runs again with the budget 15% lower. Repeating textures give up their tiling first, then the largest textures halve. Peach Gardens ends at 52% of the free pages.
- **Coordinates**: LEV positions are 16 bits, and a few courses reach further from their centre (sea and backdrop, not road). A triangle wholly past 500 CrashTeamEditor units is dropped, and a corner of one that crosses it moves to that limit (`MAX_COORD`).

### Item boxes
Each MKDS item box (object 0x65) becomes a weapon crate (`PU_RANDOM_CRATE`) on the ground under it, 8 LEV units up; one over a gap stands at the height of the ground beside it, as a crate's hitbox is only tested from a collision leaf and a leaf ends at the road. The game does not have this model on a converted track: `crate_question` is not in the shared packs, every retail track carries its own in its LEV, its textures in that track's VRM (`CTR_DEV_MODEL_DUMP` lists what a race has). So `ctr_items.py` reads it from Dingo Canyon on the player's disc:
- The model is copied byte for byte, its pointers made relative. Each texture (palette and page) it samples is decoded to a PNG the OBJ gives to a carrier block, as CrashTeamEditor packs only what a block uses. Carrier blocks sit 8 units below the course, hidden like the collision blocks.
- `lev_patch.py` finds each carrier by its bounds, reads where the image landed from the block's low texture layout, and rewrites the model's layouts to it, keeping each layout's offset in the image and the blend mode of its page.
- It then adds the model, one `InstDef` per box and the list of them. CrashTeamEditor writes neither.
- Hitboxes: retail lists each once, in the leaf that holds it. Here every collision leaf the item's box overlaps lists a copy, because a converted leaf can be a thin strip of road, and the leaf's box grows to hold it: the kart's search only reaches leaves its own box touches, so a kart in the air would miss a road leaf's items. A second hit on the same crate is stopped by its cooldown. An item that touches no leaf fails the conversion. A road leaf ends at the road and a fruit's box starts above it, so leaves are matched with 64 units of reach (`LEAF_REACH`).
- Visibility: all quadblocks share one PVS, so its instance list holds every item. `LevInstDef_UnPack` flips a list between InstDefs and Instances once per block that refers to it, so the count of blocks must be odd: when it is even, the first carrier takes a copy of the PVS with no list.

### Scenery
Objects that never move (`SCENERY` in `mkds_convert.py`: object id to model, checked against [the game's table](#objects)) are baked into the drawn mesh, through the same steps as the course. Each course keeps these models in its own archive, under `MapObj/`, textures included; an object whose model is not there is reported and left out. Objects with no model draw nothing: 0x08 and 0x0a are ambient emitters, 0x02 a value point, 0x66 a sound trigger. `FLOOR_OBJECTS` are the ones karts drive on, with a flat disc of road in the collision mesh at the height of their top, cut where it meets its neighbour so a kart has one ground: Tick-Tock Clock's four turning cogs carry the road over a pit. `collision_check.py` counts the discs as MKDS ground.

What MKDS moves to carry the road over a gap keeps a static shape in the collision, as the game's track collision is static (`add_bridges`): Delfino Square's drawbridge, each half `OPEN_BRIDGE_ANGLE` open between rails, a ramp of dash panels; Bowser Castle's slabs over the lava at their placed heights, and its turning log, whose model turns over collision of the log's own upper faces (`log_road`). Floors that turn in place are [movers](#movers); floors that travel are not planned.

What stays where it is and has no scenery shape of its own stands at its placed spot (`STILL_OBJECTS`: model, whether it is solid, how far it is lifted). A solid one gets walls round its box (`box_walls`). A placed model's materials are shared by all its copies (`bake`). A model whose origin lies in its middle stands on the ground (`GROUNDED_STILL`, and for scenery its lowest point, but for `HANGING_MODELS`, which is also where a model that karts drive on belongs: Tick-Tock Clock's gears have their road at their origin), and no solid one stands within `START_CLEAR` of the start. Waluigi Pinball's flippers are one model, mirrored by the object's first setting (`MIRRORED_BY_SETTING`).

Parts MKDS draws as one object are objects of their own here, stacked over the first (`STACKED`: the snowman's head, Pokey's body and head, a fire plant's head, which is a mover as it turns: `PakkunZHead`, a flat disc, and `PakkunMouth`, the head itself, both on the head's turns with their origin 70 over the plant). A wall painting is not crossed (`WALL_MODELS`). A scenery object is solid only where the game gives it a cylinder: a pillar of that radius and height.

An object's model is drawn 16 times its stored size, like the course, times the object's own scale. Most are one flat quad MKDS turns to the camera. Level blocks cannot turn, so a flat model is drawn as two crossed quads, seen from both sides; one quad would go edge-on as the kart passes.

Where there is road or ground under such an object, it also gets a solid six-sided pillar in the collision mesh, an eighth of its width in radius and half its height tall, so karts stop against the trunk (`pillar`). Most objects stand outside the walls where the course has no ground, like all 22 of Figure-8's trees, and get none. The course is centred on its own collision, without the pillars. Tested by driving into a pipe on `old_mario_sfc`: the kart stops 39 LEV units from its centre.

Rainbow Road's rising ring takes low walls along its open edges (`road_edges`, `EDGED_ROAD`), as its road is half as wide as a kart turns at speed. An edge is open only when no road lies `EDGE_PROBE` past it along the triangle's own slope, as neighbouring KCL triangles need not share their corners and a level test takes a join on a steep bank for a drop. A block stops a kart from its front alone, so every wall the converter builds is wound towards what it keeps (`wall_quad`).

Rainbow Road's shortcut across the last bend clips the sloped rescue surfaces to a bounded opening (`SHORTCUT_OPENINGS`). The lower fall plane still catches missed jumps.

### Objects
What an object id is comes from the game, never from what a course carries (`OBJECTS` in `mkds_objects.py`): its models from the object table in the ARM9 code (0x216b288 in the European ROM: `{id, definition, extra}`, a definition's loaders naming its model files), its hit shape from `MapObj/grpconf.tbl` in `data/MainRace.carc` (none, sphere, cylinder, box, or the object's own test). `mkds_objects.check` stops a conversion that draws an id as anything else. Speeds and timings in `MOVERS` come from each object's update code; a tick is 1/60 s.

`nitro3d.Model.triangles` runs a model's render commands, as the DS does: the matrix stack, each node's rest transform by its parent's (pivot rotations too), skinning, and vertices through the matrix at the moment they are given. A rotation is stored by columns. `lit=True` gives what has normals a colour from its material for one light. Without running them, mirrored halves collapse onto each other and skinned parts land anywhere.

### Movers
What moves in MKDS moves here (`MOVERS` in `mkds_movers.py`), as level instances, not as part of the mesh: its MKDS model, moved by `game/ConvertedTrack/Mover.c`, with CTR's own hazard code (`RB_Hazard_HurtDriver`) for what a hit does to a kart.

- **Kinds.** PATH follows the object's path in the NKM (loop, back and forth, or restart at the end, facing along it or rolling); SPIN turns about an axis, and with a cycle record speeds up, turns, slows down, stops, then the other way, as cogs do; SWING swings about one, or strokes from rest as a flipper does; ORBIT circles a centre in its own x, y plane (Wario Stadium's fire hoops: a kart passes along their z); PULSE grows out of its origin, stays, and shrinks (flame jets), and hits while more than half out; KEYS is a list of places over time; TURNS stays where it is placed and turns by a list of angles over time, about two of its axes (a fire plant's head, Waluigi Pinball's drums); STILL stays where it is placed and only hits (snowmen, piranha plants, puddles, bumpers), or only animates (chandeliers, paintings); CHOMP, LINK and GHOST aim at the player's kart, so `Mover.c` runs them tick by tick rather than from keys (below).
- **Keys.** An object with a state machine worth keeping runs in `mkds_motion.py`, tick by tick from its update code, and `MOTIONS` hands the result to `Mover.c` as places every 3 or 6 ticks. Any place can be empty: it is not there. A gate holds the clock until the race reaches a lap (lap 1 at the green light, so a delay after it counts from the start, not through the countdown), a kart comes near a point, or both, and laps can be added to it each time round, which is how an iron ball runs once a lap and a sun's flares follow it. Its objects have their own origin, as the game does, and are not lifted by a model's foot. What the game draws at random is drawn once, from a seed the object's place gives, and repeats. `CTR_DEV_MOVERS_HELD` brings out what waits or hides, to be looked at.
- **Objects that aim at the kart** run in `Mover.c` one game tick (1/60 s) at a time, with the game's numbers in world units times the course's scale, and aim at player 1's kart, as MKDS aims at its local player. A staked Chain Chomp (CHOMP; its stake is baked with the holes, `HOLES`) wanders at 1 a tick to random points 45 to 0.75 of its leash from the stake; when the kart comes within s[1] of the stake it aims once at where the kart will be (`CHOMP_LEAD_TICKS` ahead: the game's lead is not read) as far as the leash lets it, lunges for 181 ticks (pull 1/128 of the way a tick, slowed by 0.7 over 8 a tick, halved within 15), then comes back for 181 (1/1024 a tick, to within 0.75 leash less 60 times its size). Struck, it lies, then comes back. Its 7 links (LINK) hang on a sagging line from the stake's top to it. A Boo group (0x13d) is six GHOST movers: while the kart is within s[0] of the group, four show at once and two more a while later; each grows in over 40 ticks, rings the kart ahead of it (60 to 72 out, within 60 degrees of the way it goes, swaying and bobbing), lasts 300 to 450 ticks, fades over 16 and comes again. The game puts a Boo at a height that ignores the kart's, under the road on Banshee Boardwalk, so here it is over the kart. `CTR_DEV_MOVERS_HELD` rings the group's own point, for `mover_view.py`.
- **Rules of the keyed objects.** A Thwomp's path says what it does at each point: shake, hop, drop, wait on the floor, rise. It crushes while dropping (`DROPS`) and is a solid thing otherwise. Snowballs and Choco Mountain's rocks stay on the path's height, bounce on it and grow, and respawn. Waluigi Pinball's iron balls go by kind of path point, skip the stretches a path marks hidden and drop at its kind 10 and 11 points; a bumper's or flipper's point (100 and up) strikes one to 15 or 30 a tick, and it slows by 0.1 a tick to 10; a path that ends going back to an earlier point keeps the ball there for good, round the table at the course's end. A lava bubble leaps from 16 under its place at 4.7 a tick, falls back at 0.1 a tick, and hides for its s[0] ticks. From lap 2 a sun flies to each stop on its path, waits for a kart within 1200, throws two flares and flies on; flares are effects in MKDS with no model, so Wario Stadium's fire stands in for them (`FLARE_MODEL`). A Chain Chomp bounces on a flat plane at its placed height, whatever is under it; on a path its links follow it. Bats and a coffin's bats run when a kart comes near, and a Cheep Chomp leaps then too. A moving tree (0x1a3) walks its path at s[0]/100 a tick, stands each point's wait and a tick more, and walks a path that does not loop back again; a kart touching it shakes it at two frames a tick for 30 ticks in MKDS, which is left out. A fire plant turns its head to one of its paths at random (0x6d of a turn's 65536 a tick), and half the time nods, swells and fires down it, then nods back (`fire_plant_plan`); its head and its shots take their timing from one plan, drawn once, so the head points where the shot goes, and the plan turns the head back to its start before it repeats. Waluigi Pinball's drums wait until all of them stand still, then a random row of the game's table (`DRUM_SLOTS`) gives each its speed: setting 0, setting 4, or their mean; each waits, speeds up, turns, slows and creeps on to its next face, a tenth of a turn, so where the plan starts again a drum jumps by whole faces, which does not show.
- **Standing.** What is not keyed and not in `FLYING` stands on the ground by its model's lowest point, each path point settled on the collision under it (`settle`).
- **Models.** `build_model` writes a CTR model: one header, uncompressed, one strip per triangle, 8 bits a vertex per axis scaled to the model's box. A vertex's bytes are x, depth, height, and the frame's origin x, height, depth. A vertex's scratch slot is 0x57 as in retail models: lower ones overlap the colour cache. The game's front faces wind opposite to MKDS's. A model is built at its largest placed size (`largest` in `convert`), so no instance scales it up.
- **Pieces.** The game multiplies a model's 16-bit scale by its instance's (`RenderBucket_BuildM3x3`), so a model over about 2040 level units saturates: it is drawn squashed, or not at all. A model over `PIECE_MOST` is cut into `PIECE_CELL` boxes, as few as fit, its triangles cut along the planes between them (`cut_at_cells`), and each box is an instance of its own, sharing the model's textures and record list. The pieces share one vertex grid, a whole number of level units a step each way, with their origins on it, so a corner two pieces share is the same point in both (on grids of their own they rounded it apart and the course showed through the seams). The first piece has the thread: it moves, hits and carries, and puts the others where they are, from their `InstDef.rot` offsets (`_pad_scale` 1 marks them). Mover layouts have blend mode 3 (`OPAQUE_PAGE`): no mover texel sets the PS1's blend bit, and another mode cost every polygon a GPU draw of its own.
- **Near the camera.** Retail draws an instance within 4096 of the camera four times larger, for precision; a piece four times larger saturates the GTE's matrix past 510 units, so movers keep their size (`RenderBucket_NearScale`) and PGXP gives their corners precision. A piece can reach past the camera (the room a kart drives on): its box's projected corners say nothing then, so it is not culled by them, and a triangle with a corner nearer than half the projection distance is cut at that depth in model space and drawn in parts (`RenderBucket_DrawNearClipped`), where it used to be dropped. Both left holes in the floor under the camera. The PS1 GPU skips a triangle 1024 pixels wide or more (tested as at 4:3), which a piece close to the camera easily is; with what crosses the near plane cut, every mover triangle projects right, so the GPU draws a mover's whatever their size (`NativeGpu_HardwareSkipsTriangle`), as it draws the level's clipped faces. Skipped, Bowser Castle's room and Tick-Tock Clock's cogs showed black wedges up close. So a piece's polygons need not be small: cut small anyway, Bowser Castle's log drew 10,000 of them from across the course, a third of its frames over budget.
- **Between courses.** `ConvertedMover_LevelStart` allocates room for every secondary piece in the level's instance definitions and resets the per-mover lists as each level loads. The piece records live in the level's mempack alongside its instances. `CTR_DEV_CUSTOM_NEXT` races two courses in one run to check the reset. A thread's object comes from the small pool, under 0x40 bytes, which `Mover.c` asserts: a mover that gets no thread is never moved.
- **Ordering table.** Each instance drawn takes a slot in the frame's instance ordering table per 32 depth units it spans (`RenderBucket_AllocateOTRange`), and one that finds the table full is not drawn, nor is anything queued after it: the karts, their tyres and shadows and the big place number come after the level's instances. Retail's table (8 KiB for one player) fits retail's models; Tick-Tock Clock's cogs and hands filled it in most frames. The native game keeps the table in host memory at 256 KiB (`Platform_GetOTArena`), and a dev run logs its peak and how many instances found it full: the 32 courses peak under 32 KiB.
- **Drawing order.** A retail instance goes into the frame's ordering table as one block at its middle's depth, its polygons sorted among themselves only; a mover piece spans up to `PIECE_CELL`, so a cog's piece nearer than the kart on it would cover the kart, and a turning wheel's pieces under the road would cover the road. A mover's polygons instead go into the frame's table each at its own depth (`RenderBucket_SpreadsOverOT`), as the level's quads do, and in a depth-tested level they test and write depth like the level's own (`NativeGpu_AddMoverRange`), so a cog, the column through it and the road over a wheel hide each other where they are. A kart does not test depth, so a floor that carries karts goes `MOVER_FLOOR_DEPTH_BIAS` slots back, as the level's floor goes in at each quad's farthest corner.
- **Animation.** A model in `ANIMATED` plays its MKDS joint animation (`MapObj/<model>.nsbca`, read by `nitro3d.JointAnimation`: each node's translation, rotation and scale a frame, values stored every 1, 2 or 4 frames eased between). `animate` poses the model at every other tick of its loop and `build_model` writes the poses as one CTR animation of uncompressed frames over the rest frame's commands, all on one scale and origin; an animated model must fit one piece. The frame count is flagged as half frames (`HALF_FRAMES`), so the renderer eases between two stored frames on an odd one at 30 and 60 FPS alike, and a half frame is one MKDS tick: `Mover_Animate` sets the frame to the mover's clock in ticks. An even loop is stretched by a tick, as a flagged count is odd. A STILL mover loops from the tick its record's a gives (the chandelier from 60 plus up to 60 at random, as MKDS's 0x209f338; the paintings anywhere in their loop, drawn once from their place). A KEYS mover plays on its keys' clock, at its first frame while a gate holds it, unless its track says how far through its animation it is each tick (`Track.anim`, written after the keys with `MOVER_FLAG_ANIM_KEYS`): then it plays forward between keys, and on at a frame a tick while a gate holds it. The sun's speeds up by a tenth of a frame a tick to 8 while it throws its flares and slows back to 1; a moving tree's plays a frame a tick while it walks and runs on to its first frame and holds there while it stands. A TURNS mover's keys carry its animation's tick in z: a fire plant's mouth opens from 10 ticks before each shot, for 29 ticks (`fire_plant_mouth`). An animated picture cannot be baked into one layer (`composite_billboard`), so its cutouts are lifted towards the camera over its soft layers (`lift_cutouts`): the sun's face over its turning halo. A coffin is its bats (0x19e) and its own box (`COFFIN`, `coffin_lid`), whose lid opens and shuts 9 times over (369 ticks) from a kart coming within 1500. Luigi's Mansion's chandeliers and paintings are movers for it, no longer baked; the painting that hangs on a wall (`WALL_MODELS`) is not a camera-facing picture and is drawn from the front alone, as MKDS's.
- **Texture patterns.** A model with an `.nsbtp` (`nitro3d.TexturePattern`: each frame's texture and palette a material shows) shows the frame its object's code picks. A frame an instance keeps is a model of its own with those textures (`pattern_variant`, `show_pattern_frame`): a car's or truck's colour is its setting 2, every other Boo of a group shows the other face, a cow's is its setting 0, a Delfino Square onlooker's is one at random (`SCENERY_PATTERNS`). One that changes is written once with a table of texture layouts per step of its pattern, one after another, and `struct MoverPattern`, which the header's `unk1` points to (nothing reads that word; not `animtex`, as the level's model list holds mover models and `CTR_CycleTex` would run it); the renderer takes the table `ConvertedMover_TexLayout` gives as it queues the instance. A step is one set of textures, maybe mirrored. `TIMED_PATTERNS` play on the mover's clock: a Goomba its step clock's tick in each half of 40, the second half mirrored as the game draws it with its width negated (it is a flat picture); a crab's body and hands a frame every 8 ticks; a piranha plant every 16; a bat a frame a tick; a bumper on a path 1 to 29 over and over (one that stands still keeps its first). `KEYED_PATTERNS` take theirs from their keys (`Track.pattern`, y of the records after the keys): a Rocky Wrench's face changes as it goes back down. The log's last field is the step, and `mover_check.py` counts the movers whose step changed.
- **Pictures.** A flat model (`is_flat`: thin against both its sides, so not a pole such as Bowser Castle's log) takes model flag 1, which turns it round its height to the camera; its matrix must stay plain, so it keeps no facing or roll, and its hit box is as deep as it is wide. MKDS draws most pictures from both sides and winds some away from the camera, so they are built two-sided like any other material that asks for it.
- **Textures** repeat and mirror as MKDS's do: a triangle's coordinates are folded to the first tiles (`fold`), and the model's image holds as many tiles as they reach (`tiled`), at most `MAX_TILES` each way and `MAX_WIDTH` by `MAX_HEIGHT` texels. A triangle that spans more repeats than that is cut where its texture repeats (`fit_tiles`), or its corners would be clamped to the image's edge and the texture smeared into stripes: Bowser Castle's log repeats its rivets 12 times along each face, Tick-Tock Clock's pendulum and Waluigi Pinball's flippers a few. Wider than `MAX_WIDTH`, `quantize` would halve it, which its layouts would miss, so that stops the conversion. A material's texture offset is in whole textures. Images go through carrier blocks like the crate's.
- **Level data.** Each `InstDef` has `modelID` `CONVERTED_MOVER_ID`, its `model` having id -1, and in `unk28` its list in the level's `ptrSpawnType2`: see the top of `Mover.c`.
- **What a hit does** is the game's own reaction to the object (`mkds_objects.REACTION`, its reaction table for a kart in its normal state), turned into CTR's by `REACTIONS`: a wall is `SOLID`, a spin is a spin, a knock away is a spin and a push, a throw (cars, rocks, Chomps, iron balls, snowmen, Pokeys) is a blast, a crush is a squish. An object with no hit shape in the game hits nothing. What an object lets out reacts as the object the game spawns (`REACTS_AS`: bats, a fire plant's shots, a Pokey's parts; `PART_REACTS`: a sun's flares). A Thwomp is a wall that crushes from above while it drops; a moving item box breaks as a crate.
- **The mask is the Star.** A kart with the Aku Aku or Uka Uka mask is never hurt, and what MKDS does for a kart with a Star it does for it: the same reaction table's Star column (`mkds_objects.STAR_REACTION`) says what still stops it (walls, bumpers: `MOVER_FLAG_STAR_WALL`), and it rams through the rest; what the game's second table lets a Star kart strike (`mkds_objects.STRUCK`) plays the object's own struck reaction, read in its kart callback (`mkds_movers.STRUCK`, `MOVER_STRUCK_*` in `Mover.c`, numbers in world units and ticks as the game's). A Thwomp spins, slowing, until it faces its way; a Chain Chomp hops onto its back and over, and a staked one lies 120 ticks; a flare rises and spirals out; a Bullet Bill drops; a Goomba swells, shrinks away and springs back 300 ticks later; a rock, a snowball or a fire plant's shot breaks and comes with its next run; a car, bus or truck flies up turning over and lands on its path; a Cheep Cheep, a piranha plant (for good) or a crab is knocked aside spinning; a snowman bursts and builds itself again, body then head (`sman_top` is a mover for it, `STRUCK_AS`); an iron ball bounces and rolls on. A struck object hits nothing until it is itself again, but the iron ball. The reaction plays over the object's own motion (its keys run on), and holds that motion where the game's does: a Goomba and a crab stand, a staked Chomp lies. A Pokey's struck sway waits on its sway, which is not built. `ram_check.py --star` drives a masked kart into every one.
- **Hits** test the kart against a box in the mover's own axes, the game's own hit shape where it has one, else the model's box (`HIT_SHARE` narrows it), and `PENDULUM_BOB` is the pendulum's weight alone. CPU karts hit them too. `SOLID` also pushes a kart out along the box's shortest level way, so nothing catches a kart inside it; a player's kart through its sweep (`COLL_MOVED_AddCarry`), as a carry is, since a push written to its position put it under a slope (a kart driving into Waluigi Pinball's bumpers, or its iron ball rolling up the table) and it fell through the course; a floor that carries karts is never `SOLID`. A kart just hit is left alone for `MOVER_HIT_GRACE` frames: a clock hand would otherwise carry it round, hitting it again each time it recovers. The level's frame count restarts each race, so a grace further off than one grace has ended (`Mover_InGrace`). `ram_check.py` drives into every one.
- **Carrying** (`Mover_Carry`, MKDS 0x20e0764 in the object notes): a kart on a cog's or the room's top turns with it, and one on a wheel's or the log's rim rolls with it, measured from the rim's middle and only within its thickness, as the flat faces are walls; MKDS lets a kart go where the rim under it leans further than the object's limit (`RIM_FALL`: 60 degrees on the log, 85 on Tick-Tock Clock's wheels), so the log pushes a kart that does not steer against it. A player's kart is carried through its own movement sweep (`COLL_MOVED_AddCarry`), so a wall stops it; written straight to its place, the room pushed karts through walls and off the course. A CPU kart keeps to its path and is placed. A converted course's respawn point is never a kill plane: a kart on the edge of a slab touches the lava, and a respawn from it would put the kart back in for good (`COLL_FIXED_PlayerSearch_CheckMaskGrabProgress`).
- **Turning floors** (Tick-Tock Clock's four cogs, Bowser Castle's room): the road is a static disc in the collision (`FLOOR_OBJECTS`), the model turns on it, and `CARRIES` takes a kart on it round with it, position and heading. Their arms have no collision, as in MKDS. The two upright wheels at the end of Tick-Tock Clock (`test_cylinder`, `WHEELS`) are a static wheel in the collision, road where the rim faces up and walls elsewhere, so a kart can drive onto them; a wheel keeps its shape as it turns. `CARRIES` moves a kart on any turning thing about its axis: one standing up turns the kart with it, one lying down rolls it along its rim (Bowser Castle's log too).
- **Parts.** A Pokey's segments walk together. A Bullet Bill cannon moves along its path, and each `BULLET` flies straight on from where the cannon was when it restarted. A bumper with a path is `BOUND_ON_PATH`; those without are STILL. A vehicle's tyres are one axle model (`tyre_model`), stretched across and grown by its config (`TYRES`), as two pieces of the vehicle's list, front and back, that turn about their own x at MKDS's one rate as well (`MOVER_PIECE_WHEEL`, `drawn_only` 2), whatever the vehicle's speed.
- Wooden boxes (object 0x67) are CTR's Wumpa crates on the ground under them, which a kart breaks, as MKDS's are. Their model comes from the disc like the weapon crate's, and is added only where a course has any.
- Moving item boxes (object 0xc9) are weapon crates on their paths, from the path point their setting 4 names. A crate's hitbox is in the static BSP, so `Mover.c` tests the kart itself (`MOVER_ITEM_BOX`) and calls the crate's own code; that code's thread holds `inst->thread` from a break to the regrowth, so the mover's thread is kept off it.

### Translucent surfaces
A material MKDS draws at `BLEND_ALPHA` or under (Yoshi Falls' waterfalls, most of Rainbow Road) blends half and half, the only mix a level has, so its texture is brightened to MKDS's share of it (`share * 2`). `lev_patch.py` sets the palette's STP bits (a blend carrier block finds the palette, and its texture differs by a bit so it has a palette of its own), and gives every other level layout blend mode 3, which `platform/native_gpu.c` draws as opaque: a blending polygon costs a draw of its own. What is behind a blending surface shows only if it was drawn first.

A camera-facing mover with coplanar textured layers can have a solid cutout over a soft effect, as with the sun's face and halo. `composite_billboard` samples both into one texture with the cutout on top, preserving texture repeat and mirror. Both the depth buffer and the ordering table then draw a single surface with the mover's usual solid-or-clear texels.

### Pictures
`Course.picture` draws the course and its water with `mkds_view.render`, usually from the start line. Waluigi Pinball uses a view across the table with its bumpers in place because the start camera looks under the cannon ramp. `track_picture.py` writes the image as `metadata/picture.ctp`, 112x42 PS1 texels, role `picture` in the package. The Mods screen shows it for an installed package (`game/230/MM_CustomPicture.c`).

### Backdrops and water
`course_model_V` is MKDS's distant model, fixed in the world: hills, woods, a castle, Tick-Tock Clock's walls, clouds. `add_backdrop` bakes it into the drawn mesh. Where it reaches past `MAX_COORD`, what lies round the course stays in place and only what is past that closes in, on each axis, so it stays clear of the course as in MKDS, unless its one texture is a single flat colour (N64 Frappe Snowland, Choco Mountain, Banshee Boardwalk), which the level's gradient stands for at no cost in blocks. It has no collision.

The sheet of water round a course is an object in MKDS (ids in `WATER_OBJECTS`, 4 courses); `add_water` bakes its flat layer (`waterA`) in one opaque texture with the water MKDS draws under it (`waterC`, `LayeredWater`), as the see-through foam alone showed the void or sorted away as the view turned, or the moving layer where a course has no flat one, leaving out the part MKDS draws for the depth buffer alone. The water does not move, seen from both sides as a kart falls in before Aku Aku lifts it, and the sky under the horizon takes the sea's colour (or `BELOW_HORIZON`, N64 Choco Mountain's ground, where a pixel gap at a join showed white), nothing is kept under the sheet, a flat sheet stays under the lap, as at low tide: Cheep Cheep Beach's tide object is placed above its sand.

Backdrops take blocks `FAR_EDGE` times longer than the course's, and water `WATER_EDGE` times but within `WATER_NEAR` of the route (`split_water`): a block larger than the near stage's depth has all its corners far from a camera over its middle, goes down the far stage, which does not clip, and vanishes as the view turns: at road size they were over half of a course's blocks and pushed `--max-edge` up for the road too. Tick-Tock Clock costs 21% more blocks, SNES Mario Circuit 1 9%, SNES Koopa Beach 2 4%.

### Wumpa fruit
MKDS has none in races, so the converter lays them out: `fruit_rows` in `mkds_convert.py` places rows of 4, 230 LEV units apart, on the CPU route's centre line, one row every 13,000 LEV units (6 rows and 24 fruit on Figure-8; retail tracks have about 16). A row stays 800 units from the finish line and 500 from any crate, and only lies on road or boost surface with the route on the ground; a row that cannot lie where it is wanted slides forward along the route, and is dropped after half a gap. The constants are at the top of the file.

The model (`PU_WUMPA_FRUIT`, Dingo Canyon's `fruit`) is read like the crate but has no textures, so it needs no carrier block. Instances take retail's flags (`0xf`), and hitboxes retail's radius of 64 with the centre 64 above the origin (`KINDS` in `lev_patch.py`).

### Minimap
`minimap` in `mkds_convert.py` draws the KCL's road and boost triangles from above into an image. Size and shape follow the 18 retail race tracks:
- At most 96 pixels each way (retail: 64 to 96 across, 56 to 96 down).
- About 1.5 pixels across for every pixel down for the same distance (retail: 1.18 to 1.69, mostly 1.5), as screen pixels are not square.

Orientation is the owner's rule, not retail's: driving forward from the start moves the kart up the map. Of the four `UIMap` modes the converter takes the one whose up is nearest the start line's facing (`MAP_UP`), so a start running along the course's short side gives a map wider than tall (the smallest road this leaves on a race course is 84% of the best turn's, on `old_yoshi_gc`). A diagonal start has two turns as near, and takes the one that draws the road larger (`rainbow_course`). Retail has no such rule: its starts head every way on its maps, and its turns stand long tracks upright (13 of 18 have the long axis down the screen, the other 5 are within 13% of square).

A course whose infield is typed as road draws a square (GBA Luigi Circuit), so when the road covers over 1.5 times the corridor along the CPU route, it is trimmed to that corridor. Any other course skips this, so a fork the route does not take stays on its map.

The road is grown by a pixel so a thin road survives, with a white edge round a grey fill. The image goes through a carrier block like the crate's textures.

`lev_patch.py` then adds what the HUD reads:
- Icons 3 and 4 (`gGT->ptrIcons`) through the header's `levTexLookup`, both the whole image. Retail splits a map into a top and a bottom half; a `topHalfMode` of 1 draws only the bottom icon.
- A spawn table of one entry, `ST1_MAP`. Every other reader of the table checks `count` first (`CAM.c`, `MM_Title.c`), or needs a retail model a converted track does not have.
- The `UIMap`: only the ranges of its world bounds matter, so each is split round 0 to fit 16 bits. The icon start places world 0, less 4 and 2 pixels for the kart icon's corner, and 0x10 pixels for what `UI_Map_GetIconPos` takes off.
- The palette, patched in the VRM: a PS1 texel blends only with its top bit set, which CrashTeamEditor does not set. As in retail, white stays solid and the rest blends. CrashTeamEditor's palette is only as long as the image's colours and texels may follow it in VRAM, so only those three entries are touched.

The HUD shows the map or the speedometer, whichever the player picked (`hud_speedometer` in `config.ini`).

## After CrashTeamEditor
`lev_patch.py` adds what CrashTeamEditor does not write:
- The checkpoints, replacing CrashTeamEditor's (`rebuild_checkpoints`). It walks outward from the start line over neighbouring blocks, so where two sections of a course touch, the walk runs round the wrong way and leaves most of the course with no checkpoint: the game then never counts a lap and shows WRONG WAY. The nodes lie along the centre lane, at most 250 as a block's index is 8 bits. Every ground block takes the node nearest it across the ground itself, so a wall keeps two sections apart however close they are. It starts from the blocks that truly lie under a route point: a long thin triangle's box holds the road beside it, and where two parts of a lap run side by side that road took the other's node, and Aku Aku took the kart. CrashTeamEditor's own walk marks only the blocks at the finish line, which keep their nodes, and leaves its last index on whatever it reached last too, so only those within `FINISH_REACH` of the line count. Ground further from the route than 1.5 road half widths (`OFF_ROUTE`) has no checkpoint, as in retail. On a converted course a block whose node is over a quarter of a lap from the kart's progress holds that progress (`COLL_FIXED_PlayerSearch_CheckMaskGrabProgress`), where retail calls Aku Aku: MKDS never lifts a kart for a shortcut, and two parts of a lap can share a wide road. The kart drives on, its lap does not count until it is back, and a fall returns it to the last block that counted. A node's distance is in units of 8 LEV units, and a lap under 34,000 counts as that long (GCN Baby Park), since the game counts a lap when the distance jumps past 32,000.
- The collision quadblocks' faces get draw mode 3 (nothing drawn).
- The sky colours behind the bands: the clear colour (CrashTeamEditor reads it from a LEV but never writes it) and the clear split above and below the horizon (`clearColor[0]` and `[1]`, as retail levels set them). Without them anything the bands do not cover is black.
- The renderer would still walk the collision blocks, as many as the drawn ones, filling the near-camera list for nothing. The patched CrashTeamEditor puts them in their own BSP subtree (the root's second child), and `lev_patch.py` clears that subtree in every visibility list. The native renderer ignores retail visibility lists but keeps nodes that no list ever shows hidden ([LEVEL-VISIBILITY.md](LEVEL-VISIBILITY.md#the-stages)). Collision does not use the lists.
- The bot paths: a three-entry nav table, each lane a `NavHeader` and its `NavFrame`s, one more frame than points as the engine reads one past the end.
- Every collision block's normal scale (`triNormalVecBitShift` and the ten `triNormalVecDividend`s) is recomputed from the LEV's integer vertices, with the game's own arithmetic (`COLL_FIXED_TRIANGL_GetNormVec`: cross product, times the dividend, shifted). CrashTeamEditor derives them from float positions before they are rounded and gives a triblock's four triangles one shared value; on slivers a few units wide the game's normal came out as short as 1% of its length. The kart's sphere sweep measures distance with that normal, so a kart above such a sliver found itself inside it and stopped dead (an invisible wall). One shift is shared by a block's triangles, so `lev_patch.py` picks the shift whose worst triangle is closest, a too-short normal counting double; it prints the worst error over the collision blocks (0.2% on Figure-8), and the converter fails if any block is over 2%. CrashTeamEditor also rounds a block's edge midpoints to the grid, which on a thin block stands one of its triangles up; a triangle turned more than 25 degrees from the half of the block it divides gets no normal. Triangles the grid flattens to a line have no normal at all. The engine skips a triangle with no normal (`CollTriangle_HasNoNormal`, `game/COLL.c`): tested, its plane distance is 0 everywhere, and the kart stopped dead anywhere inside its block's bounds, hundreds of units from the triangle.
- The level is marked depth-tested (`LEVEL_CONFIG_DEPTH_TESTED` in `configFlags`): MKDS draws with a depth buffer, and fences, billboards and signs hang a few units in front of the walls they are on. The per-block ordering-table sort swaps those as the camera moves (flicker, a gantry's lattice drawn over its letters); a depth-tested level is drawn with a real depth test under PGXP ([PGXP.md](PGXP.md#level-depth-test)).
- It is also marked `LEVEL_CONFIG_ONE_TEXTURE_LOD`: CrashTeamEditor writes one texture layout to all four LOD slots of a texture group, so `increase_draw_distance` moves only its far clip out, not its LOD and subdivision depths ([LEVEL-VISIBILITY.md](LEVEL-VISIBILITY.md#the-stages)).
- Blocks that a far quad cannot stand for get `QUADBLOCK_FLAG_FAR_FACES` (`mark_far_faces`). Far away the renderer draws a block as one quad over its outer corners, and on the way there it slides the midpoints to the middle of the block's edges and the centre to the middle of a diagonal. That suits retail's even grids. A converted block can be uneven (a thin strip next to a tall wall, a trapezoid cut by a triangle's edge), or its faces can lie in different repeats of a texture, and the wall then bends or shows one stretched row of texels until the kart is near. `DrawLevelOvr1P_EmitFarFaces` draws such a block's four faces directly at every distance, from its nine points projected once. It costs no blocks, but four quads for one is the far renderer's largest cost, so only blocks that need it are marked: faces in different repeats, and grids that one quad would shift by more than `SHIFT_TEXELS` (`grid_shift`). At 8 LEV units of unevenness instead, 80% of Waluigi Pinball's drawn blocks were marked and its median frame took 16.0 ms; by texel shift it is 21% and 9.5 ms, Peach Gardens' castle staying straight.

`crashteameditor.patch` also gives a block's far quad the real UV of each of its four corners. CrashTeamEditor used the bounding rectangle of the block's UVs, which fits only a block aligned with its texture: on a tile that repeats it hardly shows, but on a tree or a sign the picture came apart from a distance, where the game draws a block as that one quad. It also makes the BSP builder split at the median block centre when one large block stretches a node so that every centre is on one side of its midpoint, and lets a texture fill VRAM to the last column and row (CrashTeamEditor refused an exact fit).

## Checks
The converter itself fails when `lev_patch.py` finds a drawn block with an all-zero texture layout, and when `winding_check.py` finds a drawn block CrashTeamEditor turned over: it matches every LEV block to its OBJ block by corner positions and compares the facing of the game's triangles with the OBJ normal.

`check_track.ps1` fails on any of these:
- `floor_check.py`: every collision ground point on the route's road, no kill planes or road a mover draws, top layer only, must have a drawn floor within `GAP` of it. It is what catches a kart on an invisible floor or under its own road, which every other check passes: `collision_check.py` compares with MKDS's collision, and `mkds_view.py` draws only the objects that move and the water.
- `mover_check.py` over `CTR_DEV_MOVER_LOG`: every placed mover got a thread and moved (a `STILL` one only has to run), how many hit a kart, and how many changed their texture pattern. It fails a kart that stood still for more than `LONGEST_STOP` frames: something held it.
- `ram_check.py <course>`: player 1 drives into every mover that has a hit box, one at a time, from the road behind it or head-on in the way of one that moves (`CTR_DEV_RAM`), with everything that waits brought out, and each prints what it did to the kart: hurt, pushed, a crate broken, or nothing; out of reach when it passed over or under the kart. It fails on nothing within reach. A miss can still be timing: a Monty Mole under ground, a fireball high in its arc. With `--star` the kart wears the mask, and it fails where a Star kart is hurt, pushed by what lets it through, or not pushed or striking where it should.
- `mover_view.py`: each model looked at from four sides, with no level in the way (`mover_tour.py`, `CTR_DEV_CAMERA_TOUR`, `CTR_DEV_MOVERS_ONLY`), against the same frames with no movers (`CTR_DEV_HIDE_MOVERS`). It fails a model not drawn, missing from a side, or drawn away from its place. Putting back a picture that does not face the camera, or a frame origin in the wrong order, fails it. A wall picture (`ONE_SIDED`) needs only its front half of the views.
- The custom level's render report (`[CustomTracks] level 128 ...` in the run log, written at the end of a dev run): `reserve refused`, `rendered list full` and `bsp records dropped` must be 0. `rendered list peak` is the most blocks the near-camera list held in one frame, of 256; retail Crash Cove peaks at 50, Figure-8 at 172, the passing courses at about 220 at most.
- The instance ordering table (`[CTR Dev] instance ordering table peak ...` in the run log): no instance may find it full ([Ordering table](#movers)).
- `mover_pose_check.py` over `CTR_DEV_MOVER_POSES` of a run with every mover at rest (`CTR_DEV_MOVERS_REST`): each object the map turns has the turn MKDS gives it (`mkds_convert.rotate` of its NKM rotation, which `place` writes as the entry's `rot`), its pieces where that turn puts them, and its own scale. `mover_view.py` only shows a model is drawn where the game holds it; this compares the game with MKDS. Treating Bowser Castle's log as a picture fails it.
- A crash (`crash.txt` in the run folder).
- No lap finished (`lap_check.py` over `CTR_DEV_TRACE`). The run uses `CTR_DEV_AUTOPILOT=3`: braking for corners, aiming along the line and never across the inside of a bend, and hopping along the line when wedged or fallen twice in one place, so a lap reaches every place a kart gets stuck. The script lists those places. More than `MOST_HOPS` hops fail the run; a run can pass with fewer, so read them.
- The package refused at load (`custom track ... refused` in the log, for example `duplicate pointer-map slot`): the race then runs the retail host level, and the check fails with `the race did not load the package`.
- `collision_check.py` over the in-game collision probe (`CTR_DEV_COLL_PROBE`, [DEV-TOOLS.md](DEV-TOOLS.md#environment-variables)): at every probe point the kart's own ground search runs, and a miss where MKDS has ground all around is a hole. Misses on the rim of the drivable area (`edge`), within 3 LEV units of a ground triangle (`engine edge`: retail tracks have these too, and a kart crosses a gap that narrow in a frame) and down a wall face or a steep fall surface (`wall footprint`) are listed but allowed. A point on a wall's line can land on top of the wall, so ground within 64 units of an MKDS layer 3 units away also counts. It also prints how far the game's ground is from the MKDS ground and the drawn surface from the game's ground; Every course has 0 holes; Figure-8, over 47,230 points, has ground within 3 LEV units of MKDS's and the drawn surface within 3 of the ground.

A look is judged against MKDS, never against an earlier build. `mkds_view.py` draws a course as the ROM holds it from any camera; `CTR_DEV_WARP` and `CTR_DEV_CAMERA` give the game the same view (a LEV position is `(mkds - centre) * 64 / 12`, `centre` the middle of the KCL's box, and a camera's yaw is `2048 + atan2(dx, dz)` in 4096ths of a turn). With `--objects` it draws the objects that move where the map places them, at rest; it always draws the sheet of water round the course (an object in MKDS), leaves out the rest of the objects and the sky, and which way two translucent layers stack is a guess: a real screenshot settles that. To compare an `F7` capture, take its `where.txt` kart and camera into both.

`mkds_batch.py --fps` races each course alone through `track_fps.ps1` at the owner's settings (eight karts, far camera, `increase_draw_distance`, a 1920x1080 window) and prints its median and worst frame times, the share over 16.7 ms and where its worst frames are. Frames that wait on the driver at their first clear, in `Platform_BeginScene`, count apart as driver stalls: they have nothing to do with the course. Run nothing else meanwhile. A frame's cost follows the view, not the speed, so the autopilot's lap samples it well.
