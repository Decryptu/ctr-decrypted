# Track converter: Mario Kart DS courses

`tools/tracks/` turns a course from the player's own Mario Kart DS ROM into a custom track package ([CUSTOM_TRACKS.md](CUSTOM_TRACKS.md#3-the-package-format)). Nothing from the ROM or the CTR disc is in this repository; the tools read them and write the package on the player's machine. A package holds the course's models and textures, and the crate and fruit models from the disc.

Status: all 32 race courses convert, with their item boxes, Wumpa fruit, minimap and scenery. All finish laps on autopilot; `ROADMAP.md` lists what is open. Play-testing finds visual and surface bugs the checks cannot see. Objects that move in MKDS stand still, and textures are not animated.

## Use
1. Build CrashTeamEditor's headless converter once, from an MSYS2 shell:
   `MSYSTEM=MINGW32 /c/msys64/usr/bin/bash.exe -lc "bash tools/tracks/cte/build.sh C:/t/cte"`.
   It clones [CrashTeamEditor](https://github.com/mateusfavarin/CrashTeamEditor) (MIT) at a pinned commit, applies `crashteameditor.patch`, adds `headless.cpp` and builds `C:\t\cte\cte_headless.exe`. Python needs `numpy` and `Pillow`.
2. Convert: `python tools/tracks/mkds_convert.py <rom.nds> cross_course --title "Figure-8 Circuit" --disc <ctr-u.bin>`. `--disc` is the player's CTR (USA) disc image, which the crate and fruit models come from. It writes the OBJ, LEV and probe points to `C:\t\mkds-cross_course` and installs the package in its `packages` folder, printing the pin. The same input always gives the same pin. `--store <install>\assets\tracks\packages` installs into a game folder instead.
3. Check: `tools\tracks\check_track.ps1 -Rom <rom.nds> -Course cross_course`. It races three laps on autopilot, hidden and fast-forwarded (`devrun.ps1`), and prints `PASS` or what failed. Frames land in `<work>\shots`.

`tools/tracks/mkds_batch.py <rom.nds> --disc <ctr-u.bin> --check [course ...]` does both for every race course, four races at a time, and prints a line for each; each course's output is in `convert.log` and `check.log` in its work folder. A full run takes about 30 minutes on an otherwise idle machine, and much longer beside a running game.

## What the converter writes
Scale: MKDS course models are drawn 16 times their stored size; one MKDS world unit is 1/12 of a CrashTeamEditor unit, and one of those is 64 LEV units.

**Drawn mesh** (`course_model.nsbmd`, materials with no collision flags):
- Textures are decoded from `course_model.nsbtx`. A level texel is solid or clear, so only one nearly solid in MKDS (`ALPHA_SOLID`) is solid here. A translucent decal on the floor (`find_overlays`: Waluigi Pinball's arrow panels, tree shadows) is blended in its texture over what lies under it, and if MKDS draws it in the plane of that floor it rises `OVERLAY_LIFT`, as the level is depth-tested. A wide texture that 16 colours damage (`WIDE_ROUGH`, Rainbow Road's gradients) takes 256 at half the width. A repeating texture is tiled inside its image (up to 4 times, as far as one triangle spans it) while everything fits 14 texture pages of the VRM. Textures wider than 128 pixels are quantised to 16 colours, transparency included, because a texture page holds 128 pixels at 8 bits, and a side over 256 pixels is halved, as a page is 256 texels each way. CrashTeamEditor refuses an image past either limit and gives its material an all-zero texture layout: such a block samples the framebuffer at VRAM (0, 0), so it flickers in whatever colour was last drawn there.
- Triangles are cut at texture-tile edges, then split until no edge is longer than `--max-edge` (24 units, 1536 LEV units; retail quadblocks span about 900 to 1600).
- Corners within 1.5 LEV units are welded. A corner on a surface's rim that lies on another triangle's open edge without being its corner (a tile cut one side has and the other does not) would leave a hairline of sky once both sides round to the LEV grid, so `overlap_tjunctions` moves it 1.5 LEV units across that edge, into the neighbour. The level is depth-tested, so the overlap does not show. Inserting the corner into the neighbour instead closes a little more but costs 50% more blocks on N64 Choco Mountain, where this version has 6% fewer. To measure a change, compare captures from fixed `CTR_DEV_CAMERA` positions with the kart out of shot: a racing kart does not end up in the same place in two packages.
- Two triangles that share an edge become one quad when one texture mapping covers both, the shape is convex and it is flat within 2 LEV units: the quad's midpoints and centre then get exactly the texture the triangles had. Four quads around one vertex become one quadblock, each face keeping its own UVs, when each outer edge's middle vertex is on the straight line between its corners (within 2 LEV units): far away the game draws a block as one quad over its four corners, while the neighbours keep that middle vertex as a corner, so on a curved hill the sky showed through the triangle between. What is left becomes triblocks. Fewer, larger blocks matter because the level renderer queues the blocks near the camera in a 256-entry list and does not draw what does not fit ([below](#checks)).
- CrashTeamEditor takes a block's facing from its corners 0 and 2 and turns the block over when that disagrees with the OBJ normal. A quad that undoes a midpoint split has a straight corner, which gives it a zero normal and a facing chosen at random; a block turned over vanishes from the front, as back faces are culled (the road under the kart showed black). Each quad's ring starts so that corners 0 and 2 are not straight, a grid is only made when none of its outer corners is, and the normal written is that of the diagonals.
- Materials MKDS draws from both sides (polygon attribute bit 6: fences, rails, signs, the giant blocks behind the walls) are double-sided quadblocks (CrashTeamEditor's `_drawflags`).

**Collision** (`course_collision.kcl`): invisible quadblocks carrying the terrain and flags (table `COLLISION` in `mkds_convert.py`, and `KCL_MUD` for the attribute variants that are mud or dirt, which take CTR's `Track` terrain, as slow as `Mud` without its double friction, which stops a kart that lets go of the gas; CrashTeamEditor's flag labels are misleading, the script names the game's bits). The engine tests each LEV triangle exactly, with no radius to hide gaps, so the collision mesh is made watertight before it is written. Ground a kart drives on is never `Mud`, `Water` or `Fast Water`: on those the game clips the kart's model at world height 0, where retail levels have them (`COLL.c`), and on a converted course the kart vanishes. MKDS's heavy sand (0x05) is `Slow Dirt` and its shallow water (0x11) `River Asphalt`:
- vertices within 0.5 MKDS units are welded, and a vertex lying on another triangle's edge is inserted into that edge (T-junctions leave slivers a kart falls through);
- long edges are split at their midpoints in every triangle that shares them, so neighbours stay matched (large triangles left the kart under the road);
- positions are snapped to the LEV grid (1/64 unit) before writing, so the mesh checked is the one the game tests. Corners closer than 2 LEV units are then welded: splitting two near-parallel source edges leaves midpoints closer than the first weld, and a shared corner moves the same in every triangle, so welding opens no gap;
- a triangle thinner than 3 LEV units (`SLIVER_WIDTH`) is closed: each neighbour across its longest edge is split at its far corner, so its two short edges take its place in the mesh (`close_slivers`). Dropped instead, it would leave a strip with no ground;
- a triangle the grid still turned more than 25 degrees from its source triangle (`TURNED_COS`) is dropped: turned over, the game would test it facing down, and stood up across a road it is a wall that stops the kart dead.

**Track data** (`course_map.nkm`): one lap of the CPU route, as `EPOI` holds its sections in no driving order and `EPAT` links them, forking where a course does (`cpu_route` follows each section's first link until the route closes); eight spawns behind the start, facing along the route (a kart at game angle `a` faces `(sin a, cos a)` in x/z, and `VehBirth` turns a spawn's `rot.y` a quarter turn before using it), the finish line (collision triangles crossing it are split so the lap checkpoints start exactly on it), three AI lanes along the CPU route (its centre line, and 40% of its radius to each side, on the ground, as a CPU kart holds its lane's height), a sky gradient from the most colourful texture of `course_model_V` (three bands in retail's order: band 0 highest, each from its upper edge to its lower one, the last running 100 pixels below the horizon; `CAM_SkyboxGlow` skips a band written the other way up when PGXP is off), and the probe points `check_track.ps1` uses (every 64 LEV units on every MKDS ground layer).

**Turbo pads** (KCL types 0x07 and 0x12): collision blocks of these types are listed in a `turbopad.json` preset, and CrashTeamEditor raises a copy of each 32 LEV units as its trigger (flags `0xa040`, terrain 1, as retail's). Type 0x12, MKDS's boost ramps on the jumps of Cheep Cheep Beach, Wario Stadium, Waluigi Pinball and Bowser Castle, is a super turbo pad. The kart's sphere reaches it while it drives on the road, which gives the pad's 960 reserves (`VEH_PHYS_FORCE_TURBO_PAD_RESERVES`). What the pad looks like is the MKDS road texture.

**Jump pads** (KCL type 0x0c): road, with a trigger block over each like a turbo pad's. CrashTeamEditor knows only the two turbo triggers, so `lev_patch.py` sets those blocks' terrain to `COLL_STEP_TRIGGER_JUMP_PAD_HIGH`, which a converted track reads as the game's forced high jump, the one Mystery Caves' turtles give (`VehPhysForce.c`). GBA Bowser Castle 2 and GBA Sky Garden have them, each over a gap a kart at speed clears.

### Flights
What CTR's physics cannot do, the kart is carried through on a scripted path: it has no collision, nothing can hit it (`VehPickState`), and it lands with its speed, thrown by no climb at the path's end (`game/ConvertedTrack/VehFlight.c`). `find_flights` in `mkds_convert.py` makes them from the course's data, so any course with one of these gets it, and prints each:
- **Cannons** (KCL type 0x0f, a wall with no collision): from the wall, in an arc, to the first ground on the route past the point in the NKM's `KTPC` section. `clear_arc` takes the arc of `CANNON_ARCS` that passes through least of what is drawn, roofs included.
- **Loops** (KCL type 0x13): a triangle facing up less than `LOOP_ROAD` has no collision, and the path runs on the type's surface, the kart's up its normal, so the kart goes over upside down, on until the road faces up `LOOP_LEVEL`, where the kart runs out along it. The kart keeps its own speed and its heading stays while it is not upright, so the camera does not swing at the top. The gentler parts of the type on the way in are road.
- **Gaps**: route over no ground at all for `FLIGHT_GAP` or more, past what a kart jumps.
- **Steps**: ground rising `FLIGHT_STEP` at once, which only a moving floor climbs in MKDS.

`lev_patch.py` writes them to the level's `ptrSpawnType2_PosRot`, as `VehFlight.c` describes. A flight starts at a gate, a plane the kart crosses going forward. A CPU kart holds its lane, and the lanes run along the same path (`flight_paths` in `convert`), upright and at the kart's own speed.

### CPU karts
A CPU kart follows its lane's nodes and never rises above them, so what it does comes from the lanes in `<course>_nav.json`. They lie on the ground and clear it between two nodes, where a kart runs straight, as under it the depth test hides the kart, ramps included (a ramp may rise `LANE_REACHES_RAMP` over MKDS's route), over a gap at the route's height. `flags` gives each node's `NavFrame` flags: a boost pad or ramp under a lane, looked for `LANE_SAMPLES` times between two nodes as a pad is shorter than they are apart, is a turbo or a super turbo (`LANE_FLAGS`). Off a ramp or jump pad a lane rises in the arc a kart flies, `KART_SPEED` under `KART_GRAVITY`, until it meets the ground.

### What must fit
Limits differ from course to course, and `main` and `fit_to_lev` meet them:
- **Near blocks**: the game's near-camera list holds 256 blocks (`check_track.ps1`). `MAX_EDGE` in `mkds_batch.py` gives the courses that show more of themselves at once a larger `--max-edge`: Waluigi Pinball and Airship Fortress.
- **Vertices**: a quadblock indexes vertices with 16 bits, and past 65,535 CrashTeamEditor's indices wrap and scramble its blocks. Conversion starts at `--max-edge` and raises it by a quarter until the OBJ has under 65,000 (`MAX_VERTICES`). 7 courses need this, 37.5 at most, on Peach Gardens.
- **Texture space**: when CrashTeamEditor writes no VRM, conversion runs again with the budget 15% lower. Repeating textures give up their tiling first, then the largest textures halve. Peach Gardens ends at 52% of the free pages.
- **Coordinates**: LEV positions are 16 bits, and a few courses reach further from their centre (sea and backdrop, not road). A triangle wholly past 500 CrashTeamEditor units is dropped, and a corner of one that crosses it moves to that limit (`MAX_COORD`).

### Item boxes
Each MKDS item box (object 0x65) becomes a weapon crate (`PU_RANDOM_CRATE`) on the ground under it, 8 LEV units up. The game does not have this model on a converted track: `crate_question` is not in the shared packs, every retail track carries its own in its LEV, its textures in that track's VRM (`CTR_DEV_MODEL_DUMP` lists what a race has). So `ctr_items.py` reads it from Dingo Canyon on the player's disc:
- The model is copied byte for byte, its pointers made relative. Each texture (palette and page) it samples is decoded to a PNG the OBJ gives to a carrier block, as CrashTeamEditor packs only what a block uses. Carrier blocks sit 8 units below the course, hidden like the collision blocks.
- `lev_patch.py` finds each carrier by its bounds, reads where the image landed from the block's low texture layout, and rewrites the model's layouts to it, keeping each layout's offset in the image and the blend mode of its page.
- It then adds the model, one `InstDef` per box and the list of them. CrashTeamEditor writes neither.
- Hitboxes: retail lists each once, in the leaf that holds it. Here every collision leaf the item's box overlaps lists a copy, because a converted leaf can be a thin strip of road, and the leaf's box grows to hold it: the kart's search only reaches leaves its own box touches, so a kart in the air would miss a road leaf's items. A second hit on the same crate is stopped by its cooldown. An item that touches no leaf fails the conversion. A road leaf ends at the road and a fruit's box starts above it, so leaves are matched with 64 units of reach (`LEAF_REACH`).
- Visibility: all quadblocks share one PVS, so its instance list holds every item. `LevInstDef_UnPack` flips a list between InstDefs and Instances once per block that refers to it, so the count of blocks must be odd: when it is even, the first carrier takes a copy of the PVS with no list.

### Scenery
Objects that never move (`SCENERY` in `mkds_convert.py`: object id to model) are baked into the drawn mesh, through the same steps as the course. Each course keeps these models in its own archive, under `MapObj/`, textures included; an object whose model is not there is reported and left out. The table comes from which models each course carries beside which ids it places, so check each new course's objects by eye. Objects with no model (ids 0x08 and 0x66 on Figure-8) draw nothing. `FLOOR_OBJECTS` are the ones karts drive on, with a flat disc of road in the collision mesh at the height of their top, cut where it meets its neighbour so a kart has one ground: Tick-Tock Clock's four turning cogs carry the road over a pit, and stand still here. `collision_check.py` counts the discs as MKDS ground.

What MKDS moves to carry the road over a gap is drawn and solid, standing still, as the game's track collision is static (`add_bridges`): Delfino Square's drawbridge, each half `OPEN_BRIDGE_ANGLE` open between rails, a ramp of dash panels; Bowser Castle's turning room (a `FLOOR_OBJECTS` disc), its slabs over the lava at their placed heights, and its turning log, whose collision is the log's own upper faces (`log_road`). Tick-Tock Clock's gears do not turn either. Floors that move are not planned: the engine's track collision is static.

What stays where it is and has no scenery shape of its own stands at its placed spot (`STILL_OBJECTS`: model, whether it is solid, how far it is lifted). A solid one gets walls round its box (`box_walls`). A placed model's materials are shared by all its copies (`bake`). A model whose origin lies in its middle stands on the ground (`GROUNDED_STILL`, and for scenery its lowest point, but for `HANGING_MODELS`, which is also where a model that karts drive on belongs: Tick-Tock Clock's gears have their road at their origin), and no solid one stands within `START_CLEAR` of the start. Waluigi Pinball's flippers are one model, mirrored by the object's first setting (`MIRRORED_BY_SETTING`).

Parts MKDS draws as one object are objects of their own here, stacked over the first (`STACKED`: the snowman's head, Pokey's body and head, a fire plant's head). A wall painting is not crossed (`WALL_MODELS`), a billboard no kart should stop at takes no pillar (`NO_PILLAR_MODELS`), and An id's meaning shows in where it stands: a model at a mapped id that looks wrong in `mkds_view.py`'s comparison is probably on the wrong id.

An object's model is drawn 16 times its stored size, like the course, times the object's own scale. Most are one flat quad MKDS turns to the camera. Level blocks cannot turn, so a flat model is drawn as two crossed quads, seen from both sides; one quad would go edge-on as the kart passes.

Where there is road or ground under such an object, it also gets a solid six-sided pillar in the collision mesh, an eighth of its width in radius and half its height tall, so karts stop against the trunk (`pillar`). Most objects stand outside the walls where the course has no ground, like all 22 of Figure-8's trees, and get none. The course is centred on its own collision, without the pillars. Tested by driving into a pipe on `old_mario_sfc`: the kart stops 39 LEV units from its centre.

Rainbow Road's rising ring takes low walls along its open edges (`road_edges`, `EDGED_ROAD`), as its road is half as wide as a kart turns at speed. An edge is open only when no road lies `EDGE_PROBE` past it, as neighbouring KCL triangles need not share their corners.

### Movers
What moves in MKDS moves here (`MOVERS` in `mkds_movers.py`), as level instances, not as part of the mesh: its MKDS model, moved by `game/ConvertedTrack/Mover.c`, with CTR's own hazard code (`RB_Hazard_HurtDriver`) for what a hit does to a kart. Each `Mover` names the model, how it moves, the damage (spin, blast or squish), speed and how many share its path.

- **Kinds.** PATH follows the object's own path in the NKM (loop, back and forth, or restart at the end, facing along it or rolling); SPIN turns about an axis; SWING swings about one; HOP goes out along a line and back, as an arc (fish, moles) or a slam (Thwomp, a chained Chomp); ORBIT circles a centre (bats, Wario Stadium's fire rings); PULSE grows out of its origin and hits only while out (flame jets).
- **Speeds and timings** are from the object's settings where those are understood, else `factor` and `fixed`, judged from how each one looks in MKDS: tune them in the table.
- **Models.** `build_model` writes a CTR model: one header, uncompressed, one strip per triangle, 8 bits a vertex per axis scaled to the model's box. A vertex's scratch slot is 0x57 as in retail models: lower ones overlap the colour cache. The game's front faces wind opposite to MKDS's. A model scale is 16 bits, so a big model takes the rest in its instance scale (`scale_up`). A flat model is a camera-facing billboard, unless `TWO_SIDED`. A texture repeated on a model stays on its first tile. Vehicles take their tyres in the same model (`TYRED`).
- **Textures** go through carrier blocks like the crate's, after `quantize`, as the course's do.
- **Level data.** Each `InstDef` has `modelID` `CONVERTED_MOVER_ID`, its `model` having id -1, and in `unk28` its list in the level's `ptrSpawnType2`: see the top of `Mover.c`.
- **Hits** test the kart against the model's box in the mover's own axes, so a long bus or a clock hand hits along its length. CPU karts hit them too. `HIT_SHARE` narrows a box where only part of the model is solid.
- A Pokey's segments (`POKEY_PARTS`) walk together, a fire plant shoots along its own path (`FIRE_PLANT_MOVER`), Airship Fortress' launcher stands and only its bullet flies (`BULLET_LAUNCHER`). A drum (`SOLID_STILL`) turns and keeps its walls.
- Moving item boxes (object 0xc9) are crates standing still where their path comes nearest the route, as a crate's hitbox is in the static BSP; one that no collision leaf reaches is left out.

### Translucent surfaces
A material MKDS draws at `BLEND_ALPHA` or under (Yoshi Falls' waterfalls, most of Rainbow Road) blends half and half, the only mix a level has, so its texture is brightened to MKDS's share of it (`share * 2`). `lev_patch.py` sets the palette's STP bits (a blend carrier block finds the palette, and its texture differs by a bit so it has a palette of its own), and gives every other level layout blend mode 3, which `platform/native_gpu.c` draws as opaque: a blending polygon costs a draw of its own. What is behind a blending surface shows only if it was drawn first.

### Pictures
`convert` draws the course from the start line with `mkds_view.render` (`picture_view`) and `track_picture.py` writes it as `metadata/picture.ctp`, 112x42 PS1 texels, role `picture` in the package. The Mods screen shows it for an installed package (`game/230/MM_CustomPicture.c`).

### Backdrops and water
`course_model_V` is MKDS's distant model, fixed in the world: hills, woods, a castle, Tick-Tock Clock's walls, clouds. `add_backdrop` bakes it into the drawn mesh. Where it reaches past `MAX_COORD`, what lies round the course stays in place and only what is past that closes in, on each axis, so it stays clear of the course as in MKDS, unless its one texture is a single flat colour (N64 Frappe Snowland, Choco Mountain, Banshee Boardwalk), which the level's gradient stands for at no cost in blocks. It has no collision.

The sheet of water round a course is an object in MKDS (ids in `WATER_OBJECTS`, 4 courses); `add_water` bakes its flat layer (`waterA`) in one opaque texture with the water MKDS draws under it (`waterC`, `LayeredWater`), as the see-through foam alone showed the void or sorted away as the view turned, or the moving layer where a course has no flat one, leaving out the part MKDS draws for the depth buffer alone. The water does not move, seen from both sides as a kart falls in before Aku Aku lifts it, and the sky under the horizon takes the sea's colour (or `BELOW_HORIZON`, N64 Choco Mountain's ground, where a pixel gap at a join showed white), nothing is kept under the sheet, a flat sheet stays under the lap, as at low tide: Cheep Cheep Beach's tide object is placed above its sand.

Backdrops take blocks `FAR_EDGE` times longer than the course's, and water `WATER_EDGE` times but within `WATER_NEAR` of the route (`split_water`): a block larger than the near stage's depth has all its corners far from a camera over its middle, goes down the far stage, which does not clip, and vanishes as the view turns: at road size they were over half of a course's blocks and pushed `--max-edge` up for the road too. Tick-Tock Clock costs 21% more blocks, SNES Mario Circuit 1 9%, SNES Koopa Beach 2 4%.

### Wumpa fruit
MKDS has none in races, so the converter lays them out: `fruit_rows` in `mkds_convert.py` places rows of 4, 230 LEV units apart, on the CPU route's centre line, one row every 13,000 LEV units (6 rows and 24 fruit on Figure-8; retail tracks have about 16). A row stays 800 units from the finish line and 500 from any crate, and only lies on road or boost surface with the route on the ground; a row that cannot lie where it is wanted slides forward along the route, and is dropped after half a gap. The constants are at the top of the file.

The model (`PU_WUMPA_FRUIT`, Dingo Canyon's `fruit`) is read like the crate but has no textures, so it needs no carrier block. Instances take retail's flags (`0xf`), and hitboxes retail's radius of 64 with the centre 64 above the origin (`KINDS` in `lev_patch.py`).

### Minimap
`minimap` in `mkds_convert.py` draws the KCL's road and boost triangles from above into an image. Size and shape follow the 18 retail race tracks:
- At most 96 pixels each way (retail: 64 to 96 across, 56 to 96 down).
- About 1.5 pixels across for every pixel down for the same distance (retail: 1.18 to 1.69, mostly 1.5), as screen pixels are not square.

Orientation is the owner's rule, not retail's: driving forward from the start moves the kart up the map. Of the four `UIMap` modes the converter takes the one whose up is nearest the start line's facing (`MAP_UP`), so a start running along the course's short side gives a map wider than tall (the smallest road this leaves on a race course is 84% of the best turn's, on `old_yoshi_gc`). A diagonal start has two turns as near, and takes the one that draws the road larger (`rainbow_course`). Retail has no such rule: its starts head every way on its maps, and its turns stand long tracks upright (13 of 18 have the long axis down the screen, the other 5 are within 13% of square).

A course whose infield is typed as road draws a square (GBA Luigi Circuit), so when the road covers over 1.5 times the corridor along the CPU route, it is trimmed to that corridor. Any other course skips this, so a fork the route does not take stays on its map.

The road is grown by a pixel so a thin road survives, with a white edge round a grey fill. The image goes through a carrier block like the crate's textures.

`lev_patch.py` then adds what the HUD reads:
- Icons 3 and 4 (`gGT->ptrIcons`) through the header's `levTexLookup`, both the whole image. Retail splits a map into a top and a bottom half; a `topHalfMode` of 1 draws only the bottom icon.
- A spawn table of one entry, `ST1_MAP`. Every other reader of the table checks `count` first (`CAM.c`, `MM_Title.c`), or needs a retail model a converted track does not have.
- The `UIMap`: only the ranges of its world bounds matter, so each is split round 0 to fit 16 bits. The icon start places world 0, less 4 and 2 pixels for the kart icon's corner, and 0x10 pixels for what `UI_Map_GetIconPos` takes off.
- The palette, patched in the VRM: a PS1 texel blends only with its top bit set, which CrashTeamEditor does not set. As in retail, white stays solid and the rest blends. CrashTeamEditor's palette is only as long as the image's colours and texels may follow it in VRAM, so only those three entries are touched.

The HUD shows the map or the speedometer, whichever the player picked (`hud_speedometer` in `config.ini`).

## After CrashTeamEditor
`lev_patch.py` adds what CrashTeamEditor does not write:
- The checkpoints, replacing CrashTeamEditor's (`rebuild_checkpoints`). It walks outward from the start line over neighbouring blocks, so where two sections of a course touch, the walk runs round the wrong way and leaves most of the course with no checkpoint: the game then never counts a lap and shows WRONG WAY. The nodes lie along the centre lane, at most 250 as a block's index is 8 bits. Every ground block takes the node nearest it across the ground itself, so a wall keeps two sections apart however close they are. It starts from the blocks that truly lie under a route point: a long thin triangle's box holds the road beside it, and where two parts of a lap run side by side that road took the other's node, and Aku Aku took the kart. CrashTeamEditor's own walk marks only the blocks at the finish line, which keep their nodes, and leaves its last index on whatever it reached last too, so only those within `FINISH_REACH` of the line count. Ground further from the route than 1.5 road half widths (`OFF_ROUTE`) has no checkpoint, as in retail. On a converted course a block whose node is over a quarter of a lap from the kart's progress holds that progress (`COLL_FIXED_PlayerSearch_CheckMaskGrabProgress`), where retail calls Aku Aku: MKDS never lifts a kart for a shortcut, and two parts of a lap can share a wide road. The kart drives on, its lap does not count until it is back, and a fall returns it to the last block that counted. A node's distance is in units of 8 LEV units, and a lap under 34,000 counts as that long (GCN Baby Park), since the game counts a lap when the distance jumps past 32,000.
- The collision quadblocks' faces get draw mode 3 (nothing drawn).
- The sky colours behind the bands: the clear colour (CrashTeamEditor reads it from a LEV but never writes it) and the clear split above and below the horizon (`clearColor[0]` and `[1]`, as retail levels set them). Without them anything the bands do not cover is black.
- The renderer would still walk the collision blocks, as many as the drawn ones, filling the near-camera list for nothing. The patched CrashTeamEditor puts them in their own BSP subtree (the root's second child), and `lev_patch.py` clears that subtree in every visibility list. The native renderer ignores retail visibility lists but keeps nodes that no list ever shows hidden ([LEVEL-VISIBILITY.md](LEVEL-VISIBILITY.md#the-stages)). Collision does not use the lists.
- The bot paths: a three-entry nav table, each lane a `NavHeader` and its `NavFrame`s, one more frame than points as the engine reads one past the end.
- Every collision block's normal scale (`triNormalVecBitShift` and the ten `triNormalVecDividend`s) is recomputed from the LEV's integer vertices, with the game's own arithmetic (`COLL_FIXED_TRIANGL_GetNormVec`: cross product, times the dividend, shifted). CrashTeamEditor derives them from float positions before they are rounded and gives a triblock's four triangles one shared value; on slivers a few units wide the game's normal came out as short as 1% of its length. The kart's sphere sweep measures distance with that normal, so a kart above such a sliver found itself inside it and stopped dead (an invisible wall). One shift is shared by a block's triangles, so `lev_patch.py` picks the shift whose worst triangle is closest, a too-short normal counting double; it prints the worst error over the collision blocks (0.2% on Figure-8), and the converter fails if any block is over 2%. CrashTeamEditor also rounds a block's edge midpoints to the grid, which on a thin block stands one of its triangles up; a triangle turned more than 25 degrees from the half of the block it divides gets no normal. Triangles the grid flattens to a line have no normal at all. The engine skips a triangle with no normal (`CollTriangle_HasNoNormal`, `game/COLL.c`): tested, its plane distance is 0 everywhere, and the kart stopped dead anywhere inside its block's bounds, hundreds of units from the triangle.
- The level is marked depth-tested (`LEVEL_CONFIG_DEPTH_TESTED` in `configFlags`): MKDS draws with a depth buffer, and fences, billboards and signs hang a few units in front of the walls they are on. The per-block ordering-table sort swaps those as the camera moves (flicker, a gantry's lattice drawn over its letters); a depth-tested level is drawn with a real depth test under PGXP ([PGXP.md](PGXP.md#level-depth-test)).
- It is also marked `LEVEL_CONFIG_ONE_TEXTURE_LOD`: CrashTeamEditor writes one texture layout to all four LOD slots of a texture group, so `increase_draw_distance` moves only its far clip out, not its LOD and subdivision depths ([LEVEL-VISIBILITY.md](LEVEL-VISIBILITY.md#the-stages)).
- Blocks that a far quad cannot stand for get `QUADBLOCK_FLAG_FAR_FACES` (`mark_far_faces`). Far away the renderer draws a block as one quad over its outer corners, and on the way there it slides the midpoints to the middle of the block's edges and the centre to the middle of a diagonal. That suits retail's even grids. A converted block can be uneven (a thin strip next to a tall wall, a trapezoid cut by a triangle's edge), or its faces can lie in different repeats of a texture, and the wall then bends or shows one stretched row of texels until the kart is near. `DrawLevelOvr1P_EmitFarFaces` draws such a block's four faces directly at every distance, from its nine points projected once. It costs no blocks, but four quads for one is the far renderer's largest cost, so only blocks that need it are marked: faces in different repeats, and grids that one quad would shift by more than `SHIFT_TEXELS` (`grid_shift`). At 8 LEV units of unevenness instead, 80% of Waluigi Pinball's drawn blocks were marked and its median frame took 16.0 ms; by texel shift it is 21% and 9.5 ms, Peach Gardens' castle staying straight.

`crashteameditor.patch` also gives a block's far quad the real UV of each of its four corners. CrashTeamEditor used the bounding rectangle of the block's UVs, which fits only a block aligned with its texture: on a tile that repeats it hardly shows, but on a tree or a sign the picture came apart from a distance, where the game draws a block as that one quad. It also makes the BSP builder split at the median block centre when one large block stretches a node so that every centre is on one side of its midpoint, and lets a texture fill VRAM to the last column and row (CrashTeamEditor refused an exact fit).

## Checks
The converter itself fails when `lev_patch.py` finds a drawn block with an all-zero texture layout, and when `winding_check.py` finds a drawn block CrashTeamEditor turned over: it matches every LEV block to its OBJ block by corner positions and compares the facing of the game's triangles with the OBJ normal.

`check_track.ps1` fails on any of these:
- `floor_check.py`: every collision ground point on the route's road, no kill planes, top layer only, must have a drawn floor within `GAP` of it. It is what catches a kart on an invisible floor or under its own road, which every other check passes: `collision_check.py` compares with MKDS's collision, and `mkds_view.py` draws no objects.
- `mover_check.py` over `CTR_DEV_MOVER_LOG`: every placed mover got a thread and moved.
- The custom level's render report (`[CustomTracks] level 128 ...` in the run log, written at the end of a dev run): `reserve refused`, `rendered list full` and `bsp records dropped` must be 0. `rendered list peak` is the most blocks the near-camera list held in one frame, of 256; retail Crash Cove peaks at 50, Figure-8 at 172, the passing courses at about 220 at most.
- A crash (`crash.txt` in the run folder).
- No lap finished (`lap_check.py` over `CTR_DEV_TRACE`). The run uses `CTR_DEV_AUTOPILOT=3`: braking for corners, aiming along the line and never across the inside of a bend, and hopping along the line when wedged or fallen twice in one place, so a lap reaches every place a kart gets stuck. The script lists those places. More than `MOST_HOPS` hops fail the run; a run can pass with fewer, so read them.
- The package refused at load (`custom track ... refused` in the log, for example `duplicate pointer-map slot`): the race then runs the retail host level, and the check fails with `the race did not load the package`.
- `collision_check.py` over the in-game collision probe (`CTR_DEV_COLL_PROBE`, [DEV-TOOLS.md](DEV-TOOLS.md#environment-variables)): at every probe point the kart's own ground search runs, and a miss where MKDS has ground all around is a hole. Misses on the rim of the drivable area (`edge`), within 3 LEV units of a ground triangle (`engine edge`: retail tracks have these too, and a kart crosses a gap that narrow in a frame) and down a wall face or a steep fall surface (`wall footprint`) are listed but allowed. A point on a wall's line can land on top of the wall, so ground within 64 units of an MKDS layer 3 units away also counts. It also prints how far the game's ground is from the MKDS ground and the drawn surface from the game's ground; Every course has 0 holes; Figure-8, over 47,230 points, has ground within 3 LEV units of MKDS's and the drawn surface within 3 of the ground.

A look is judged against MKDS, never against an earlier build. `mkds_view.py` draws a course as the ROM holds it from any camera; `CTR_DEV_WARP` and `CTR_DEV_CAMERA` give the game the same view (a LEV position is `(mkds - centre) * 64 / 12`, `centre` the middle of the KCL's box, and a camera's yaw is `2048 + atan2(dx, dz)` in 4096ths of a turn). It leaves out objects and the sky, and which way two translucent layers stack is a guess: a real screenshot settles that.

`mkds_batch.py --fps` races each course alone through `track_fps.ps1` at the owner's settings (eight karts, far camera, `increase_draw_distance`, a 1920x1080 window) and prints its median and worst frame times, the share over 16.7 ms and where its worst frames are. Frames that wait on the driver at their first clear, in `Platform_BeginScene`, count apart as driver stalls: they have nothing to do with the course. Run nothing else meanwhile. A frame's cost follows the view, not the speed, so the autopilot's lap samples it well.
